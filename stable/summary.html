Summary dashboard ​

A one-page overview of the headline results for readers with limited time. Every number, table and figure on this page is produced by the same model fit as the full Analysis and refreshes whenever the data updates. See the Analysis page for the methods, assumptions and supporting detail behind each result, and the Sensitivity page for the forecast validation, the outbreak size implied by each data stream, the comparisons with McCabe et al. and Chamla et al., and the delay and tree-prior sensitivity analyses.

Reduced data streams. The situation reports dropped the daily new-suspected-case tile on 6 August 2026. The count continues from the alert-validation table that replaced it, which is the same construction under a new layout, so the series resumes on 7 August. The 6 August report is left out, its alert table being internally inconsistent. The treatment-centre patient-movement table was dropped on 3 August, and although the reports returned to their full format on 12 August with per-province prose covering much of the same ground, that stream stays frozen pending a decision on whether the two are comparable. Every other stream continues, so the most recent weeks rest on fewer streams than earlier ones. See the inclusion rules in data/README.md.

Headline estimates ​

  • Cumulative infections: the outbreak is estimated to have caused 30% 12646–14922, 60% 11430–15394, 90% 9947–18687 infections to date, reported and unreported.

  • Against the 7258 laboratory-confirmed cases by the cut-off that is roughly 1.4–2.6× as many infections, so confirmed cases are estimated to capture only a small share of the outbreak.

  • Outbreak start and age: the outbreak is estimated to have begun on a start date of 30% 2026-03-18–2026-03-20, 60% 2026-03-15–2026-03-21, 90% 2026-03-10–2026-03-24, an elapsed age to the cut-off of 30% 177–179, 60% 176–182, 90% 173–187 days.

  • Growth rate and doubling time: the initial growth rate is estimated to have been 30% 0.044–0.058, 60% 0.044–0.067, 90% 0.037–0.082 per day, an initial doubling time of 30% 15.6–12.0, 60% 15.7–10.4, 90% 18.7–8.5 days. The latest growth rate is estimated to be 30% 0.016–0.03, 60% 0.005–0.035, 90% -0.013–0.057 per day, a latest doubling time of 30% 43.6–23.1, 60% 144.1–19.8, 90% -53.1–12.2 days.

  • Reproduction number: the initial reproduction number is estimated to have been 30% 1.18–1.28, 60% 1.14–1.36, 90% 1.14–1.54 and the latest to be 30% 1.08–1.1, 60% 1.02–1.16, 90% 0.94–1.31.

  • Case-fatality ratio: the case-fatality ratio is estimated to be 30% 0.44–0.48, 60% 0.43–0.51, 90% 0.37–0.56.

Estimated reproduction number ​

The time-varying reproduction number R(t), the average number of further infections caused by each infection. A value above one means the outbreak is growing.

The same trajectory by province, with the national one in grey behind each panel. A panel tracking grey says that province moves with the national trend.

Provinces ​

Modelled infections by province, and each province's size, reproduction number and relative case ascertainment.

Infections over time ​

Modelled cumulative infections, symptom onsets and deaths. These are the underlying outbreak, upstream of the testing and reporting that produce the observed counts, so they are larger than the reported cases.

Reported cases: model versus observed ​

Modelled reported cases against the observed reported cases over time, a check that the fit reproduces what was seen on the ground.

Reproduction number by data stream ​

The reproduction number each data stream implies on its own, fitted to that stream alone. Agreement between the streams supports the joint estimate; disagreement shows where they pull in different directions.
